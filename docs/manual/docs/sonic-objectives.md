**Releasing the musical imagination** {#IMAGINATION}

The *Learning Manual* covers a broad selection of individual processes. While much of it is concerned with the mechanics of using the software, the various topics indirectly suggest a number of different sonic goals for a composition. While composing with sound is discussed in a couple of other places in *A Learning Manual*, this document discusses such objectives in general terms, with a broad brush, as it were, and also goes on to suggest specific CDP processes relevant to various goals. It is always the aim to become fluent with the mechanics so as to release the imaginative capacity.

The deeper issue is "what is it all for?" i.e., connecting sound transformation processes with the goals of a composition, connecting them to one's own creative impulse. This document inevitably presents my own personal outlook on making these connections. You will no doubt have your own similar or different! approach.

In practice, it is seldom the case that a single operation will realise an imagined sound. The process is open-ended, one result leading to another idea, and a constant sculpting of the sonic material. Successful and favoured chains of processes can be saved as command line batch files, SoundLoom 'instruments', or Soundshaper 'Patches'.

One place to start may be the documents and books listed in the Learning Manual's document [*CDP Primary Reference Materials*](cdp-primary-reference-materials.md), which is part of Topic 1. I find Robert Fraser's *CDP Guide* to be especially helpful because it provides an overview of the whole set of CDP software. I also often refer to the *CDP Desk Reference* to find the name of a program I can't quite remember, to see quickly what processes in a certain area are available, or to check on input files and modes. When figuring out what to do next in a composition, perusing the *CDP Guide* and/or the *CDP Desk Reference* can be helpful in finding a way forward. The two (non-CDP) books listed there are also gold mines of information about working with sound: David Sonnenschein's *Sound Design* and Curtis Roads' *Composing Electronic Music*.

It is also worth looking at companion software for the CDP System. Rajmil Fischman has written some powerful software for mixing, granular textures and spatial movement. Check out the LINKS page on the CDP Website. Another companion package is *ProcessPack*, coded by Richard Orton with creative input from myself. *Csound* is a major package for the electronic synthesis of sound, but can also handle soundfiles and has powerful score-building facilities, among many other features. Hybrid setups that include DAWs such as *ProTools* are common, as mentioned earlier. The list is in fact endless, and you will have your own favourites.

**A sea of music** {#MUSICALSEA}

Music is vast. The variety of musical styles is astounding, especially when world music is taken into consideration. Navigating this sea is both exciting and daunting. Creating new even more daunting. Highly relevant are personal listening preferences and experiences, and one's own view and categorization of different musical styles. I have never looked back since a summer course in 20th century music I enjoyed at Peabody Conservatory in Baltimore. We had to listen to 10 compositions a week in their listening room for 6 weeks – the 60 compositions covered most of the important music written in the 20th century and gave me aural memories that have been a mainstay of my work as a composer. Listening with attention improves the ability to think in the medium of sound. But even more important is attending to the silence within, one's inner sea from which one's own music will emerge. My first and formative composition teacher teacher Edgar Curtis used to advise attentive inner silence when stuck with a composition. It is amazing what flows out of the depths.

So in the end it comes down to one's own experiences, biology, outlook, aspirations, preferences. Being aware of these and being true to these lies at the heart of the creative endeavour – and usually presents the biggest challenge. Paolozzi: "Follow your preferences." It also helps to develop a sense of worth in one's own individual voice, surrounded as we are by the marvels produced by others.

**An inner motion** {#RHYTHMICFEEL}

Music is a time art and at its heart lies movement and flow. A piece will often begin with the feeling of a certain kind of rhythm which will greatly affect how the composition unfolds. The shaping of 'passage': *the illusion of flowing time* (Suzanne Langer's term in *Feeling and Form* discussed in [*About Composing with Sounds*](about-composing-with-sounds.md#PASSAGE)) is the essential process that makes the music what it is. This can emerge from and be similar to what the artist is feeling at the time, or it can be something quite different from current personal feelings, an objective construction that the artist shapes with intent and assesses as the first listener.

**Casting a net over the sea** {#CASTTHENET}

The composer casts his or her net over a sea of possibilities, musing, dreaming, looking for something that might stand out. The following are in no particular order.

- **rhythms:** gentle, relaxed, rocking, lively, incisive, pressing, accelerating, insistent, powerful, dance, additive, vocal syllabic, interlocking ...
- **harmonies:** sonorous, gritty, rapidly changing or relatively static (harmonic rhythm), building in complexity, conventional chords and extended tonal practice, new harmonic configurations, harmonic rhythm, harmonic fields, pitch structures based on serial technique, on early music, or on a particular harmonic progression ...
- **figures or sonic units:** a key to much musical development is the use of figures/motifs, and how they are handled: repeated, varied in many different ways. In electroacoustic music, the analogue to this is sonic transformation: changing the timbre or character of the sound, mingling features of different sounds, layering sounds. There are myriad ways to do this. These figures or sonic units can be used gesturally or to create (evolving) textures
- **melodies:** much music makes use of thematic melody ('signature tunes', which often have a particular rhythm as well). So far, there hasn't been too much of this in electroacoustic music, but attention-grabbing sonic gestures may serve the same role. Folk, aria, bel canto, recitative, *sprechstimme*, inspired by raag or maqam, monody, chant, melodic arcs and intricate intervallic designs in Renaissance polyphony ...
- **intervals:** the felt quality of a pitch interval, such as the difference between a minor second and perfect 5th. The feel of intervals seems especially significant in serial music, which I tend to experience as a 'poetry of intervals'.
- **counterpoint:** how much happens at the same time, and how much do various components have their own independent behaviour? contrapuntal density, imitation, layering generally, the use of ground bass or passacaglia ...
- **information flow:** how quickly do things change over unit time and how different and dense are the sonic events? This is a key question in the art of time. ...
- **texture:** developed in many different ways in 20th century music, texture-building has become a major part of electroacoustic composition, filling vertical and horizontal space with multi-events of various kinds – what kind of space, what density, whether repetition of sonic units or a collage of sounds, how rhythmic ...
- **timbre:** relationships built almost entirely from timbral features. This of course is the nuts and bolts of electroacoustic music: accessing and playing with the inner structures of sound. While much is new in how things are done, there are deeper levels of continuity with existing musics, such as matters of rhythmic feel and motion, density, variation etc. – see [*About Composing with Sounds*](about-composing-with-sounds.md#TIMEPATTERNS).
- **form:** seems to grow out of a deeper philosophical outlook – repetitive, constantly and organically evolving, returning to the starting point (but transformed), building to a major critical moment, essentially static with little change, alternating contrasting sections, symmetrical arrangements of sections, variations on an initial melody, motif or sound, making use of numerical proportions ...
- **phrase joins:** cadence and new phrase, overlap, continuous flow, with a break ...
- **phrases:** regular lengths, irregular lengths, nonexistent, ...
- **spatial:** spatial placement and movement, such as call and response, alternating choirs of voices or instruments, placement of acoustic instruments in a concert hall, placement of sound in background, foreground, high or low, movement across a spatial area (panning) ...
- **improvisation:** playing with ideas on one's favoured instrument ...
- **movement and stasis:** holding steady in one voice while another moves vigorously ...
- **favourite passages:** remembering certain passages of music that have made a strong impression ...
- **technical features:** perhaps drawn to certain musical techniques or areas calling for innovation ...
- **beliefs and concerns:** musing on personal deep-seated beliefs and concerns about things going on in the world, sometimes inspiring, sometimes evoking anger and sadness ...
- **death & life:** the inevitable, a personal bereavement, relationships, warfare, biodiversity, ongoing species extinction, planetary renewal (with or without us) ...

**Extending an idea** {#EXTENDING}

Once something stands out in this sea of possibilities, the next step is to consider how to extend it, or rather, to consider how *it* would like to evolve and grow. One thinks for example of 'motive spinning', subject + countersubject & episodes in the Baroque era, theme & passage work and balanced phrases in the Classical period, harmonic expansiveness in the Romantic period, organic development of initial figures (in a way, a mosaic technique), the 20th century preference for forms that continuously evolve, the exploration of an O-R-I-RI serial matrix with its built-in redundancy, rhythmic repetition in so-called minimal music. These established formal procedures may subtly inspire ways to extend a broader sonic palette.

However, extending material in electroacoustic music presents its own challenges and opportunities. When pitch and sometimes rhythm have minor roles, the emphasis is even more on the timbral qualities of sound, on the character of the 'surface' of the sound (smooth or rough), on processes of timbral modulation and the mingling of different sounds. Texture and a certain degree of randomness are also key elements as one is often if not usually dealing with sounds without the clear edges of conventionally pitched material.

**Specific sonic objectives** {#SUGGESTIONS}

Having said all that, here are *some* 'broad brush' suggestions for composition objectives. They exemplify how I review available resources, seeking to kickstart the process of imagining what might be done to transform a sound, and more specifically, to find a way into the range of CDP functions that might be helpful in realising unique sound design objectives. The command line names are given in the list below to make it easier to look them up in the Reference Documentation. Every composer is different. It is fascinating to observe how varied the resulting compositions can be!

- **CDP System Functions** on the command line – these are built into the GUIs:
    - see soundfile directory listing - DIRSF
    - make a copy of a soundfile to convert from one format to another or just to ensure correct wav/CDP header: COPYSFX (part of Richard Dobson's Multi-channel Toolkit), which also handles Surround and Ambisonic formats, with related support facilities in the MC Toolkit
    - play a soundfile or analysis file: PVPLAY (include the extension with the name)
    - record from Line In: RECSF

- **Basic editing** (command line or GUIs):
    - **analysis & resynthesis**: PVOC
    - SFEDIT Suite for cut & keep: CUT, excise: EXCISE, insert: INSERT, and join: JOIN
    - HOUSEKEEP Suite for channel extraction, mono to stereo & *v.vs.*: CHANS, change sample rate or other properties: RESPEC, gated extraction: GATE and TOPNTAIL2
    - SNDINFO Suite for maximum sample level: MAXSAMP, find length: LEN
    - MODIFY Suite for alter amplitude level: GAIN, apply panning: SPACE, add reverb/echo: REVECHO and STADIUM ECHO
    - **Soften the start/end edges of a sound**: ENVEL DOVETAIL, TOPNTAIL, and, with gating, the standalone TOPNTAIL2

- **Mix/assemble sounds**:
    - **Create a mixfile**: for vertical and timeline mixing in the horizontal plane, or a multi-channel mixfile to place sounds on a multi-speaker rig – via a text editor or facilities in one of the GUIs
    - **mix** with the SUBMIX SUITE: MIX, MERGE, MERGETWO, MERGEMANY, STACK (and note the mix utilities such as ADDTOMIX, BALANCE, FADERS, FILEFORMAT and GETLEVEL)
    - **multi-channel mix** with the MULTI-CHANNEL SUITE: NEWMIX MULTICHAN to mix and spatially place multi-channel soundfiles
    - Also see TEXTURE

- **Timing and Rhythm**:
    - soundfile entries in a mix are set by their respective start times in a mix file
    - **Loop** a sound with or without overlaps: EXTEND LOOP
    - **Stretch** a sound in time like pulling on a rubber band: STRETCH TIME, STRETCH SPECTRUM
    - **Rhythms** specified with TEXTURE TIMED and with fully-defined motifs in TEXTURE MOTIFS, TEXTURE TMOTIFS and TEXTURE ORNATE (PRE- and POST-), and create a timing template for whole groups of note events with TEXTURE TGROUPS
    - **Sample-Hold** effects with EXTEND FREEZE
    - **Timed repetitions** of a sound with EXTEND SEQUENCE, and including transpositions with EXTEND SEQUENCE2
    - **find and re-time silence-separated events**: RETIME
    - **make a click track**: SYNTH CLICKS
    - **Set timing and density** in the TEXTURE Suite with the *packing*, *tgrid* and *skiptime* parameters
    - Also see the Table Editor in *SoundLoom*, Data->Data Editor in *Soundshaper*, or COLUMNS on the Command line: for many ways to manipulate numerical data. Use function `9 Manipulate Columns  > separate columns` to extract columns of data from a multi-column file and save them separately. Accessing these facilities can be useful when working on data sonification.

- **Transform tonal qualities**:
    - **Time-Domain transposition** to make a sound deeper and longer, or higher and faster: (MODIFY SPEED)
    - **Segmentation** to break up a sound into pieces, scatter and rearrange: DRUNK, LOOP, RANDCHUNKS, RANDCUTS, SCATTER, SCRAMBLE, INTERLEAVE, SHUFFLE, WEAVE
    - **Reduce a sound to a ghost of itself**: HILITE TRACE and BLTR ('Blur-Trace')
    - **Massage the tonal quality of a sound:** the FILTER Suite programs: LOHI, BANK, USERBANK, VARIABLE, VARIBANK ...
    - **Smudge a sound** with the BLUR Suite: AVRG, BLUR, CHORUS, NOISE, SPREAD, SUPPRESS
    - **Grungy sounds**: the DISTORT Suite, which manipulates portions of soundfile defined by zero-crossings – 'wavecycles'. A demonstration of (increasingly) grungy sounds is included in the CDP Demo: 'DiceDemoHighlights'. This is a pared down version of 'DiceDemo' and replaces the Grainmill demo with 'Grunge Factory', all the sounds are made by programs from the DISTORT Suite.
    - **De-emphasise or emphasise** with the HILITE Suite (BLTR, FILTER, GLISTEN, PLUCK, TRACE), REPITCH EXAG
    - **Fade a sound into itself**: FILTER ITERATED
    - **Reverse** a sound: MODIFY RADICAL Mode 1: Reverse
    - **Modulate** a sound: MODIFY RADICAL Mode 5: Ring Modulate, Mode 6: Cross Modulate
    - **Granulate a sound and manipulate the grains**: GRAIN Suite, BRASSAGE, (PC: *Grainmill*); MODIFY SAUSAGE
    - **Roughen the surface of a sound**: whole DISTORT package; BRASSAGE, GRAIN Suite, PC: *Grainmill*; and see 'Segmentation'

- **Work with pitch/frequency**:
    - **Extract and save the pitch trace of (any) analysis file**: REPITCH GETPITCH, use with the COMBINE programs
    - **Transpose**: MODIFY SPEED, REPITCH TRANSPOSE, REPITCH TRANSPOSEF, REPITCH COMBINE using pitch traces or transposition (.trn) files
    - **Create glissandi**: (time-varying MODIFY SPEED, TRANSPOSE, STRANGE SHIFT, ACCU, SUPERACCU, STRANGE GLISS, STRANGE WAVER)
    - **Create arpeggios**: (ARPEG, time-varying pitch parameter on Harmonic Field of a TEXTURE program)
    - **Add vibrato (frequency oscillation**: REPITCH VIBRATO, STRANGE WAVER) or **tremolo** (amplitude oscillation: ENVEL TREMOLO)
    - **Tune sounds** to specific pitches/chords with the PITCH Suite (ALTHARMS, CHORD, CHORDF, TUNE, TUNEVARY), FILTER Suite (BANK, BANKFRQS, USERBANK, VARIBANK, VARIBANK2)
    - **Snap to a defined harmonic grid**: TEXTURE Suite
    - **Tune sustained spectral bands**: SUPERACCU
    - **Filter sweeps through a frequency range**: FILTER SWEEPING

- **Play with envelope shapes**:
    - **Amplitude envelope**: ENVEL Suite: esp. EXTRACT, IMPOSE, REPLACE, REPLOT, RESHAPE; SUBMIX CROSSFADE
    - **Spectral envelope**: REPITCH Suite: GETPITCH, ANALENV, COMBINE; FORMANTS VOCODE; SPECROSS
    - **Envelope shape wavecycles**: i.e., portions of soundfile defined by zero crossings: DISTORT ENVEL

- **Create multi-event textures** (for best results it is important to prepare sounds for use with the imagined textures, e.g., attack envelope, tonal qualities etc.):
    - **Sound entry control**: repeated entry of the same sound or randomly selected sounds, or the timed entry of several sounds ('sounds' parameter, which can be time-varying): TEXTURE SIMPLE
    - **Strong sustained tones** created with TEXTURE SIMPLE using carefully timed entry of sounds with long splices at beginning and end
    - **Aural analogues to visual shapes** made with time-varying pitch and packing (density) parameters, using TEXTURE SIMPLE; and with specified rhythms if use fully-defined motifs as below
    - **Harmonic dimension**: See 'Work with Pitch' for adding a harmonic dimension to a texture – Harmonic Field/Set option available in most of the TEXTURE Set programs
    - **Create textures of 'fully-defined' motifs**: pitched-level-controlled and with defined rhythms: TEXTURE MOTIFS, TMOTIFS, and ORNATE (PRE- and POST-)
    - **Create melodic layers/canons**: TEXTURE ORNATE (PRE- POST-) which builds on a 'nodal substructure'

- **Intermingle sounds in various ways**:
    - **Move between spectral features**: MORPH BRIDGE, MORPH GLIDE
    - **Morph between sounds**: MORPH MORPH (spectral features), NEWMORPH NEWMORPH (between peak channels), NEWMORPH NEWMORPH2 (between dissimilar spectra, potentially with a harmonic flavour)
    - **Varied combinations of spectral files**: COMBINE Suite: CROSS, DIFF, INTERLEAVE, MAX, MEAN, SPECTWIN, SUM
    - **Create combinations of pitch and transposition files**: COMBINE, COMBINE2 (see REPITCH GETPITCH to extract a pitch trace)
    - **timed switches between several sounds**: SFEDIT SPHINX or SFEDIT TWIXT
    - **Work with resonant frequencies** with the FORMANTS Suite: GET formants, GETSEE formants, PUT formants. Also see VOCODE
    - **Extract formants from one sound and impose on another**: VOCODE
    - **Convolve two sounds**: FASTCONV (listed in the COMBINE Suite, but an independent program)
    - **Impose channel amplitudes of one sound onto another**: SPECSPHINX, with an option to set the proportion of frequencies to inject into the output spectrum
    - **Interpolate partials of one sound towards those of another**: SPECROSS PARTIALS
    - **Create several intermediate soundfiles** between two sounds: SUBMIX INBETWEEN

- **Work with vocal sounds**:
    - **Varied facilities**: FORMANTS Group; SFEDIT SYLLABLES; HILITE VOWELS; REPITCH VOWELS;
    - **Work with pitch-synchronous grains (FOFs)**: the PSOW Suite
    - **Weird transformations of a voice**: the DISTORT Suite
    - **Other tonal variants**: See 'Transform tonal qualities' above

**Concluding thoughts** {#CONCLUSION}

The processes listed above by no means include everything available in the CDP Software, and there are many more software packages available. It's rather "how long is a piece of string?". Hopefully, this document provides some help in connecting process with compositional purpose.

My concluding thoughts would like to emphasise two things.

1. **Re-sculpting** – It is seldom that a desired result will be achieved in one step. CDP does not aspire to 'black box' one in - one out solutions. Rather, a constant re-sculpting of a sound is what usually happens: honing the sound by repeatedly feeding one output into another process.

2. **Determining the overall form** – There are many types of overall form, and many views on what these should be. However, it is worth, I believe, invoking a certain amount of critical faculty regarding one's own creation. Do passages drift from one to another with no real connection (or clearly-defined contrast)? Does material cohere? What is the scaffolding on which coherence is built? Is interest maintained by sufficient introduction of new sounds and shapes? Is there an overall form, or is there a collection of varied passages? Do passages follow one another with a musical 'logic' (sense that that is what should come next)? If there is an overall form, is it what you want? Does the music maintain vividness and a certain element of surprise? Does the music *end* (have a sense of completion) or just drift away?

Nobody said composition was easy.
