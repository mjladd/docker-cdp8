**Some types of sound as possible sources** To help get the mental wheels oiled and turning for working with sounds, the following list sets out some possible raw materials. Listen for the overall tone and harmonicity, the attack and decay transients, the spectral content at different frequency levels, and noise elements. All these affect both what processes they might respond to best, meaning how they can be further developed, and how they might fit into your composition plan.

Note that sounds will inevitably have an extra-musical dimension that need to be taken into account unless perhaps all source recognition has been removed and sound is only used as an abstract morphology. Also, each sound is going to respond differently to a sound transformation process. Also see David Sonnenschein, *op. cit.*

- **sharp metallic** – (bell, gong, edge of cymbal or metal sheet): ring, clang
- **soft metallic** – (cymbals, thunder sheet): bash, clank, twang, wobble
- **soft and burbly** – (waterfall, waves, wind, leaves, paper, engines, fire, rainstick): rustle, purr, ripple, roar
- **animal voices** – (cats, dogs, birds, pigs, elephants, lions, whales, dolphins, crickets, frogs, imaginary ..., difference between mating calls and warning calls): tweet, warble, intone, roar, purr, bark, click, hiss
- **the human voice** – (both words and non-verbal exclamations): speak, mumble, groan, shout, cry, laugh, exclaim, whisper, declaim, sing
- **drum beats** – (wood, skin, log, claves): beat, bounce, incisive, soft, hypnotic
- **sounds with lots of noise content** – (white & pink noise, thunder sheet, shouts, roars, motors, traffic, guiro): rasp, scrape, bash [can be good sources for tuning effects]
- **natural sonic textures** – (such as thousands of frogs calling or crickets , traffic, footsteps, morning chorus, waterfalls, wind in trees, waves on the beach, seagulls, geese on the wing ...)

**Towards greater understanding of sound in a creative context** {#REFERENCE}

To dig deeper into the nature, implications and possibilities of sounds, I have found much inspiration in David Sonnenschein's text *Sound Design - The Expressive Power of Music, Voice, and Sound Effects in Cinema* as listed among the Primary Reference Materials.

**Recording sounds** {#RECORD}

Recording your own sounds can help you start with exactly the kind of sound you need. A good portable digital recorder, field microphone (and wind shield!) is all that is needed – and a bit of luck that you can avoid the inevitable passing car or plane in the distance. If some sonic cleanup is needed, CDP has the SPECNU tools such as CLEAN that removes persisting signal that falls below a threshold. A good commercial package is *RX7-Elements*.

**Sound Libraries** {#LIBRARIES}

Another source of sounds is one or the other of the sound libraries that are commercially available, or sounds that can be found on the Internet for free or modest per-sound cost. An Internet search will turn up a mesmerising variety of options. Here are some suggestions for sourcing sounds:

- **BBC sound effects:** https://sound-effects.bbcrewind.co.uk – need a licence to use commercially; Pro Sound Effects operates the commercial licensing of BBC sound effects.
- **British Library Sounds Archive** https://sounds.bl.uk – these appear to be available for listening, but not for download and private use
- **Csound** http://www.csounds.com – sounds made via the *Csound* synthesis engine
- **De Wolfe Sound Effects** https://www.dewolfemusic.com – thousands of pro sounds to buy and download (some recorded by U/Keele's Cliff Bradbury)
- **Epidemic Sound** https://www.epidemicsound.com/sound-effects – seems aimed at use in films or broadcasting media; not sure how much one sound would cost and what the terms would be
- **Freesound** – free, Creative Commons Licensed sounds
- **Macaulay Library:** https://www3.macaulaylibrary.org (at the Cornell Lab of Ornithology – not free, but excellent quality and service)
- **Pro Sound Effects** – commercial purchase, but 100% royalty free
- **Royalty Free Sound Effects Library:** https://www.freetousesounds.com/sound-library –
- **Zapsplat** https://www.zapsplat.com – they retain the copyright but make them available copyright free without limitation

However, watch out for copyright restrictions. Also, ensure that the sound quality is adequate for your purpose, which is not always the case. I am trying to find out more about the feasibility of using sounds with a Creative Commons licence in one's own sound transformation work. The 'ShareAlike' term says: "If you remix, transform, or build upon the material, you must distribute your contributions under the same license as the original." In case this would apply to the whole of an electroacoustic composition in which such material was used, I have so far avoided using Creative Commons material.
